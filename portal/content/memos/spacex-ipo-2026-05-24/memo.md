## The thesis in five lines.

- **Launch cadence is the cash machine**, not Starlink. Reusable booster turnaround under 14 days inverts the unit economics that public-market comps still assume.
- **Starlink steady-state ARPU is the swing variable.** We model three scenarios bracketed by spectrum allocation and direct-to-cell rollout.
- **DoD concentration is mispriced risk.** A material share of FY25 revenue runs through a single counterparty whose budget process is, structurally, political.
- **What the All-In pod got wrong:** conflated Starship test pace with revenue acceleration, and ignored launch-license throughput as a binding constraint.
- **Fair-value bracket** assumes a 2027 listing window, with a 35% range driven primarily by the Starlink ARPU scenario.

> [!pull]
> The DoD line is the line nobody on the pod priced. Concentration of that magnitude through a single political customer is, by definition, a multiple-compressor.
