// what it does

Built for solo GTM — and the work that won't do itself.

01

Knows your product cold.

Ingests every signal — site, docs, market chatter, your own input — and builds a product context that every downstream skill uses. The level of understanding a great fractional CMO would have after a deep-dive week. In minutes.

02

Picks your ICP and your shots.

Identifies the buyers most likely to pay. Writes the positioning. Maps the competition. Builds a channel plan with specific actions, sequencing, and metrics — not generic "do content marketing" advice.

03

Catches moves before they hurt you.

Watches your competitors and the incumbents in your buyer's stack. When something material changes, it tells you what to update in your positioning, plan, and pricing — and shows the cascade.

// in practice

One product, all seven skills.
A fictional SaaS — "Loop" — runs through every step below.

PRE-FLIGHT
VALIDATE
gtm-smoke-test
I have an idea — Loop, a tool that auto-drafts a solo founder's weekly newsletter from their work. Smoke test it on FB Marketplace before I build.

Listed three angle variants on Facebook Marketplace at $29/mo and watched the engagement for 5 days.

  • "Founder weekly, written for you" — 47 saves, 18 messages, 4 attempted purchases
  • "Stop ghosting your list" — 31 saves, 9 messages, 1 attempted purchase
  • "Newsletter on autopilot" — 12 saves, 2 messages, 0 attempted purchases

Verdict: real demand. The "guilt" angle clearly outperforms the "automation" angle. Build at $29 entry price; pivot copy to the founder-weekly framing.

STEP 01
BUILD
gtm-onboard-ingest
Onboard Loop. Site at loop.so, docs at loop.so/docs. I'm targeting solo founders writing weekly newsletters.

Ingested the site, docs, your two LinkedIn posts, and the relevant subreddits. Built the product context.

What Loop is: an LLM that reads a founder's GitHub, Linear, and calendar each week, then drafts a newsletter in their voice.

What's distinctive: work-in-real-time drafting (not retro summaries), founder-voice match, sub-10-minute weekly edit loop.

What's underdeveloped in your messaging: the "voice match" claim is the strongest moat — your site buries it on page 3.

STEP 02
BUILD
gtm-position-segment
Find Loop's best ICPs and write positioning. Use the product context.

Researched four ICP candidates. Two have clear pull, two are distractions.

  • ICP-1 — devtools founders writing weekly — high buying power, already pay for Beehiiv, hate writing. Best fit at $29/mo. Lead with this.
  • ICP-2 — fractional CMOs running newsletters for clients — willing to pay $99/mo for multi-user, but smaller market.
  • Skip: solopreneur creators (mass market, low willingness to pay) and B2B SaaS marketing teams (need approvals you don't support).

Wrote up a positioning doc with a messaging matrix and a pricing recommendation ($29 / $99).

STEP 03
BUILD
gtm-competitive-landscape
Map Loop's competitive landscape — direct, adjacent, and the incumbents in my buyer's stack.

Six players matter. Two are real threats, four are noise.

  • Direct: Castmagic AI Newsletter (weak product, strong distribution) — real threat. Tugan AI — niche, ignore.
  • Adjacent / buyer-stack incumbents: Beehiiv shipped "AI assist" three weeks ago — watch closely; Substack hasn't moved.
  • Indirect: ghostwriters on Upwork still take ~40% of the budget for this ICP. Your real competitor is human, not LLM.

Mapped the full landscape — pricing, community sentiment, and a feature-watch list for Beehiiv.

52 tools
STEP 04
BUILD
gtm-plan
Build a GTM plan for ICP-1. Channels, sequencing, what to do week 1.

Four channels in priority order. Two are owned (run them now), two are leveraged (start week 3).

  • Week 1–2 (owned): 20 founder DMs, 1 product-hunt-style launch post, weekly newsletter showing Loop using itself.
  • Week 3+ (leveraged): sponsorship in 2 founder newsletters ($800 total); guest on Indie Hackers podcast.
  • Skip: SEO (12-month payback, you don't have the runway), paid ads (CAC will exceed LTV until pricing is validated).

Built the full plan — success metrics, weekly cadence, and the "decline to run" reasoning per channel.

STEP 05
MAINTAIN
gtm-competitive-monitor
What changed in Loop's competitive landscape this week?

Three changes since last week's scan. Two are signal, one is noise.

  • Signal: Beehiiv shipped voice-cloning for AI assist. Closes part of your moat. Worth a reassess.
  • Signal: Castmagic dropped their newsletter price from $39 to $19. Aggressive — they're feeling the squeeze.
  • Noise: Tugan AI raised a $2M seed. Doesn't change their product trajectory in your buyer's eyes.

Compiled a delta report with screenshots and the trigger to run gtm-reassess on Beehiiv's launch.

STEP 06
MAINTAIN
gtm-reassess
Beehiiv just shipped voice cloning. What do I update in my positioning and plan?

This is material. Three documents need updates — here's the cascade.

  • positioning.md: the "voice match" claim alone is no longer defensible. Reframe around the work-context ingestion (GitHub, Linear, calendar) — that's still uniquely yours.
  • gtm-plan.md (ICP-1): kill the LinkedIn ad copy that leads with voice match. Replace with the "writes from your actual work, not your vibes" angle.
  • pricing: hold at $29 for now. Re-evaluate in 60 days based on Beehiiv adoption signal.

Prepared an impact assessment with section-level diffs. Approve and I'll apply the changes.

// built on curunir

A minimal agentic harness. In your house.

The seven skills above are one pipeline on Curunir — a deliberately minimal agentic harness. The same engine runs every step, and nothing more. /launch/ is one pipeline; the same harness can run research, scheduling, or anything else you wire into it.

It runs on a small device in your home or office. The language models live in the cloud — but your roadmap, your customer list, and your competitive intel don't. They stay on the device.

Reachable from anywhere — web, CLI, email — through a thin portal that brokers the conversation without copying the data.
