SpaceX IPO — bull case vs bear case.

A working note on the SpaceX listing. Launch cadence economics now scale as a true exponential, but the structural risk in a DoD-concentrated revenue mix is being underweighted by every public commentator on the cap table — including the All-In hosts.

Read the PDF → Download ↓ Share on X →
SUMMARY

The thesis in five lines.

  • Launch cadence is the cash machine, not Starlink. Reusable booster turnaround under 14 days inverts the unit economics that public-market comps still assume.
  • Starlink steady-state ARPU is the swing variable. We model three scenarios bracketed by spectrum allocation and direct-to-cell rollout.
  • DoD concentration is mispriced risk. 41% of FY25 revenue runs through a single counterparty whose budget process is, structurally, political.
  • What All-In got wrong: conflated Starship test pace with revenue acceleration, and ignored launch-license throughput as a binding constraint.
  • Fair-value bracket assumes a 2027 listing window, with a 35% range driven primarily by the Starlink ARPU scenario.
"The DoD line is the line nobody on the All-In pod priced. Concentration of that magnitude through a single political customer is, by definition, a multiple-compressor."

Full sensitivity tables, the comp set, the All-In transcript with our annotations, and the fair-value bridge. Sources are footnoted; every cited transcript link is live.
