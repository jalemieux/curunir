# Variant Angles

Variants must sit on **different positioning axes**, not just differ in wording. Reworded copy tests nothing; it adds noise proportional to the sample size. Real angle tests reveal which buyer mental model the product actually activates — often surprising the builder.

## The Four Default Axes

Use these for the default 4-variant run. Each axis frames the same product through a different buyer motivation.

### 1. Functional — what it does

Describes the product in terms of its mechanism and output. The buyer is sold on capability.

- Hook style: specs, features, use cases
- Who it lands with: buyers already solution-aware, comparing options
- Example (AI coach box): *"On-prem AI server. Gemma 4 26B running locally. Always-on coaching assistant. No cloud, no subscription, no logs."*

### 2. Emotional — how it makes you feel

Describes the felt experience of owning / using the product. The buyer is sold on an emotional state.

- Hook style: mood, relief, companionship, pride
- Who it lands with: buyers in pain, buyers seeking a feeling more than a feature
- Example (AI coach box): *"Some nights you just need someone to help you think clearly. This sits on your desk and does exactly that. No apps, no login — just a conversation."*

### 3. Identity — who you become

Describes the kind of person who owns / uses it. The buyer is sold on self-image.

- Hook style: aspirational, lifestyle, tribe-signaling
- Who it lands with: buyers for whom ownership says something about who they are
- Example (AI coach box): *"For people who think better by thinking out loud. Your own AI interlocutor, yours alone — nothing leaves the house."*

### 4. Pain / Avoidance — what it helps you escape

Describes the bad thing the product prevents. The buyer is sold on relief / fear reduction.

- Hook style: before/after, escape-from, cheaper-than
- Who it lands with: buyers who have a specific pain they're actively trying to solve
- Example (AI coach box): *"Therapy is $300/hour. ChatGPT logs everything. This is neither. One-time cost, in your house, yours forever."*

## Extended Axes (for runs with more than 4 variants)

Pull from these when testing 5+ variants, or when one of the default axes is clearly wrong for the product.

### 5. Social / Status

How other people will perceive the owner. Works for visible / conspicuous products.

- Example: *"The setup every serious AI person has on their desk right now."*

### 6. Contrarian / Against-the-grain

Positions the product as a rebellion against a prevailing norm or incumbent.

- Example: *"Stop renting your own brain back from OpenAI. Run your coach on hardware you own."*

### 7. Scarcity / Urgency

Positions availability or timing as the lever, not the product itself.

- Example: *"Small production run. Hand-assembled. When they're gone, they're gone."*
- Caution: scarcity framing on a fake-door can over-trigger buyers; filter inbound for genuine interest vs FOMO response.

### 8. Proof / Social validation

Foregrounds what other people are saying or doing with it.

- Example: *"Three founders I know replaced their executive coach with one of these."*
- Caution: only use if you can credibly imply existing users without misleading buyers beyond the fake-door contract.

### 9. Process / How it works

Leans on the story of *how* the product works — often a novel mechanism is itself the hook.

- Example: *"Built on Gemma 4 26B. Runs on a €400 mini-PC. Responses in under 2 seconds, fully air-gapped."*

---

## How to Pick Axes for a Run

1. Read the idea one-liner and the builder's own framing. That framing is usually **one** axis — probably functional or pain.
2. Pick 4 axes that are **each different from each other** AND **at least one different from the builder's default framing.** This is where surprises come from.
3. Match the axis to the venue audience. Marketplace buyers respond well to Pain/Avoidance and Functional; Identity framing underperforms there but crushes on Twitter / Nextdoor.

---

## Anti-patterns (Reject These as Variants)

- Four titles that differ only by synonym swaps ("AI Coach" vs "AI Mentor" vs "AI Advisor" vs "AI Guide") — this is copy testing, not angle testing.
- Variants where the axis isn't clearly nameable. If you can't say "this one is the pain variant," it isn't one.
- Variants that combine two axes ("emotional + identity"). Pick one per variant; axis combinations dilute the signal.
- Using price to differentiate variants. Price is a run-level knob. To test price, do a second run.
